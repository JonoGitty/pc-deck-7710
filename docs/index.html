Build your own 1-DIN car head unit.

One portable C renderer drives real glass, a browser preview and a PC music visualiser — the same code, the same dots, on all three.

What this is

A deck is what a head unit is called. This one started on a PC and grew into the real thing.

The PC deck — working

A Pioneer-style faceplate in a browser, fed by whatever your PC is playing. Spectrum analyser, VU needles, album art, synced lyrics, dolphins when the music stops. This is what most people run.

The car deck — renderer done, firmware not

ESP32 and an OLED or VFD panel, Bluetooth audio from your phone, into a standard DIN cage. The renderer is finished and verified, and the firmware is written and compiles — A2DP, metadata, the analyser, both panels, movies and diagnostics. It has never run on hardware, and this site will say so until it has.

Everything on this site was rendered by the code in the repository. The screen animations come out of the same C the firmware links; the faceplate photographs are the real page in a real browser with the WebSocket stubbed; the drawings are generated from the firmware's own pin definitions and from measurements in JSON. There are no mockups here, and one command regenerates the lot — which is the only way a site like this stays true a year from now.

The build guide

Thirteen chapters, in the order you do them. Each one has a next.

This used to be one long page and it was the wrong shape: somebody standing at a bench had to scroll past dolphins to find step 6, with no way to tell how much was left. So it is a sequence now — the eleven mechanical steps are a page each, with the drawing for that step and nothing else on it, and every page says where you are in the whole.

And the rest of it

The parts of the project that are not a build.

The screens

Ten display modes — analyser, VU, scope, waterfall, album art, synced lyrics, and the dolphins — every dot drawn by the same C.

Calls, and the radio

Hands-free over HFP with the call state derived rather than tracked, and an FM/AM tuner whose audio never enters the microcontroller.

The animations

Baked movies at 10 fps, made from a Python scene, a GIF or a video — and why importing footage of another head unit never works.

Try it now

Run the PC deck, or compile the renderer to WASM and preview a panel you have not bought yet.

How it holds together

One renderer compiled twice, and the differential test that keeps the C honest against the JavaScript it was ported from.

Status, honestly

What works, what is written but unproven, and what has never met the hardware it is written for.

If you build one you are the manufacturer, and every consequence is yours — including fire, battery drain, airbag circuits, driver distraction, insurance and type approval.
