Chapter 2

Your car. The deck is the same object in every car — what changes is a bag of adapters, and where the amplifier goes.

Fitting is standard: an ISO 7736 cage and an ISO 10487 harness, which is the entire reason a home-built head unit drops into anything. Answer three questions before you buy: does it fit, what does it need on top, and where is the amplifier going to live.

⚠️ The deck has no amplifier, and you need one. Its output is line level — what an amplifier expects and what a speaker does nothing useful with. On most cars the head unit is the amplifier, so there is nothing already in the dash to feed. A TDA7850 or TDA7388 4-channel board is £12–20 and is the same IC a real head unit contains. It needs its own fused 12 V feed, not the deck's 5 V buck, and it must share one ground point with the ESP32 or you get alternator whine.

Or don't buy one at all: the donor you gutted already contains that amplifier, bolted to its own heatsink and wired to the ISO connector — the reuse route, and it is £0.

And a bare mux has no volume control. Fit a PT2313 / TDA7313 audio processor (£2–4) instead and you get source selection plus volume, bass, treble, balance and fader over the I²C bus the tuner already uses — no extra pins, and it gives two back.

Where the amplifier lives is car-specific

In a small roadster it is genuinely hard, and the answer for a mid-engined MR2 is not the answer for an MX-5. A 2-DIN aperture is the way out: deck in the top half, amplifier board in the bottom half — front end and amp in one box, which is exactly what a factory head unit is.

Nine entries in full, with harness and aerial adapters, depth and per-market notes: VEHICLES.md, or python3 tools/deckctl.py fit s2000 ap1. The S2000 has no steering-wheel controls at all — no interface box, no learning step. Adding your car is one JSON file.

⚠️ The radio region follows where you drive, not where the car was built. A JDM import in Britain receives British stations. Japan's FM band is 76–95 MHz against Europe's 87.5–108, and the Americas use 10 kHz AM spacing against 9 kHz — get it wrong and most of the band is missing or mistuned. Five regions, stored in NVS, set once.

Adding your car is one JSON file in vehicles/ and a pull request. If a field does not apply — many cars have no steering-wheel controls at all — say so explicitly: “none fitted” is information, a missing key is not.
