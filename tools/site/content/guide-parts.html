Buy the parts. About £35 gets a working deck on a desk; the car tier roughly doubles it.

The authoritative list, with quantities and search terms, is BUILD.md. What follows is what the money goes on and which items people get wrong.

WhatWhich oneWhy this one
The brainESP32-WROVER-E, 16 MB flash WROVER for the PSRAM, original ESP32 for Bluetooth Classic. Not the S3
The panelSSD1322 256×64, or Futaba GP1294AI Both have drivers. The VFD is the authentic look; the OLED is the easier build
The DACPCM5102A board I²S in, line level out, no configuration pins to get wrong
Source switchingPT2313 / TDA7313 (preferred) or 74HC4052 The mux only selects. The PT2313 also gives you volume, bass, treble, balance and fader over I²C — and hands back two GPIOs
The tunerSi4735 module FM/AM with RDS over I²C, analogue audio straight out — it never enters the ESP32
The amplifierTDA7850 / TDA7388 board — or the donor's The deck's output is line level. Reusing the donor's is £0 — chapter 5
ButtonsTactile switches and a rotary encoder The buttons go on one analogue pin as a resistor ladder, not one pin each

Check the chip before you solder to it. deckctl doctor reads the chip ID over USB and tells you what is missing from your toolchain — and it catches an ESP32-S3 bought by mistake, which is the single most expensive error available at this stage, because everything about it looks right until Bluetooth audio does not exist.
