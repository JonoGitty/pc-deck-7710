Pair it, tune it, load it. The last hour, and the settings that are set once and then forgotten.

Pair the phone

The phone's own Bluetooth menu. No app and no pairing code. Once paired it reconnects on ignition, and metadata — title, artist, album, and the album art if the phone sends it — arrives over AVRCP without anything else being configured.

Set the radio region — once

⚠️ The region follows where you drive, not where the car was built. A JDM import in Britain receives British stations, so it wants the European band plan. It is not a subtle failure: Japan's FM band is 76–95 MHz against Europe's 87.5–108, so a European deck driven in Japan can tune about a tenth of the band, and the Americas use 10 kHz AM spacing against 9 kHz elsewhere — on the wrong step every station lands between channels.

Five plans — EU, UK, US, JP, AU — stored in NVS. Changing it re-clamps the saved frequencies and every preset into the new plan, so a preset cannot survive as a frequency the region cannot reach.

Volume, and where it comes from

If you fitted a PT2313, the encoder is your volume control and the chip does bass, treble, balance and fader as well — all over the I²C bus the tuner already uses. If you fitted a bare 74HC4052 mux instead, there is no volume control anywhere in the deck: the mux only selects a source. In that case the encoder falls back to panel brightness and the volume lives on your phone. It is worth £2–4 not to have that be true.

Load what it plays

python3 tools/deckctl.py movies          # choose what it plays
python3 tools/deckctl.py pictures *.jpg  # your own photographs

Movies are baked animations at 10 fps, packed into flash — the bundled ones are the ocean, the touge run, the solar system and the AE86. Making your own is its own page, and the short version is: procedural if it must react to the music, baked if it just has to look good.

Steering-wheel controls, if the car has them

A resistor-ladder input on its own pin, with a learning mode: hold each wheel button in turn and the deck records the voltage. Many cars in the fitment list have none at all — the S2000 has no wheel controls in any market, which means no interface box and no learning step. That is one of the things worth knowing before buying an adapter for it.
